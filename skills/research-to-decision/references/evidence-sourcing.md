# Evidence Sourcing

## Never fabricate a citation

If a claim can't be verified with an available tool, either don't make the
claim, or mark it explicitly: "Assumption (unverified): ..." Fabricating a
plausible-sounding source is worse than admitting the limit of what could
be checked.

## Prefer primary sources

Official documentation, the project's own benchmarks/release notes, and
primary technical specs are more reliable than aggregator blog posts
summarizing them — aggregators can be stale or wrong about version-specific
details. When only a secondary source is available, say so.

## Watch for staleness

Version numbers, pricing, and feature availability change. If a source
might be outdated (no visible date, or a date more than ~1 year old for a
fast-moving product), flag that explicitly rather than presenting it as
current fact.

## Separate vendor claims from independent evidence

A vendor's own performance claims about their product are evidence, but
weaker evidence than an independent benchmark or a third-party post-mortem
of production usage. Note which kind a given claim is.

## When evidence conflicts

If two sources disagree, say so and explain which is weighted more heavily
and why (recency, independence, specificity to the actual use case) rather
than silently picking one.
